Your domain is **connected**, not transferred. It stays with your current registrar, and your existing website keeps working exactly as it does now.

## Before you begin

- The login for your domain registrar
- About 15 minutes; DNS changes can take a while to take effect

## How it's set up

Your pages publish on a subdomain — for example, `pages.yourpractice.com`. You add one record at your registrar that points that subdomain to PracticeOS.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Add the domain in PracticeOS">
    Add `pages.yourpractice.com` as a domain in your account. PracticeOS shows you the record to create.
    {/* VERIFY: menu path and button labels for adding a domain; the exact record value shown. */}
  </Step>
  <Step title="Create the record at your registrar">
    At your registrar, add a CNAME record for `pages` with the value PracticeOS gave you. Every registrar's screens look different; look for "DNS" or "DNS records".
  </Step>
  <Step title="Verify">
    Back in PracticeOS, verify the domain once the record is saved.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Why a general subdomain

Use a general name like `pages.` rather than one named after a single offer. You'll publish more than one page over time, and they can all live on the same subdomain.

## Linking pages to the domain

A page is linked to your domain after the page exists. If you're setting up the domain before any pages are built, that's fine — the link is made later.
